The idea sentence

Lessons 1 and 2 were about what to take away. This one is about what to put in: an idea only your subject could fill. By the end you’ll write one sentence that tells you what a page should look like, and check it with the competitor test.

Why a sentence comes first

In Taste Skill’s study of 247 sites, what separated the best from the forgettable wasn’t styling: it was a concept taken from the thing itself. The lowest-scored sites had no idea beyond the standard formula (Taste Skill, “Find the idea”). Your rule T1 makes it a step you can’t skip: write the sentence before building.

On this page, [something that belongs to the subject] becomes [a design device].

Taste Skill’s example: a bakery named after a street with seven doors. “A loaf of bread becomes the background” could be any bakery. “The seven doors of our street become the page’s seven sections” could only be this one.

The competitor test

Put the nearest real competitor into your sentence. If it still works, you’ve described the category, not the subject. Rewrite. “Clean and modern” fails every time: it’s a style, and anyone can have it.

Where to look

Start from an inventory: 8–12 things unique to the subject (places, numbers, materials, rituals, artefacts). Then look for the first blank in one of these:

Look at its…AskExample
OutputWhat does it produce? Put the real output on the page.A camera that prints poems sets its copy as paper slips
Object or shapeWhat does it look like? Enlarge it, crop it, make it the frame.A chair’s silhouette as the page’s one motif
NameCan the letters hold a photo, stack, split?A name set as a wall of letters
PlaceIs there one view that makes you want to be there?The room the guest wakes up in, full screen
RitualIs there a sequence the page can perform as it scrolls?The road a shipment really takes
ArtefactWhat printed thing already exists in its world?A ticket, menu, label, receipt or manual
OppositeWhich other category owns the feeling this one lacks?A bank that borrows from a field guide

You’ve already used one: the Food tab is a printed menu (an artefact), with dotted leaders and scores.

One live thing

T1 also asks for one live thing: something the visitor can do, or that’s different each visit, made from the idea. Taste Skill reports every winning page in its blind tests had one, and jurors named it first. The rules for it:

  • It uses the subject’s real content.
  • It works on a phone, with a thumb.
  • It has a plain fallback without scripts.
  • It never blocks reading.
  • It’s one thing, not a playground.

Practice

1. “On this bakery’s site, a loaf of bread becomes the background.”

  1. Fails, any bakery fits
  2. Passes, bread is specific
  3. Fails, it needs photos
  4. Passes, it mentions food

Put the bakery two streets away in the sentence: it still works. That’s the category talking.

2. “On this studio’s site, minimalism becomes the layout.”

  1. Passes, minimalism is a choice
  2. Fails, studios need big photos
  3. Fails, it is a style
  4. Passes, layouts can be ideas

The first blank must come from the subject, not from taste. Minimalism belongs to everyone, like “clean and modern”.

3. A camera that prints poems sets its website copy as paper slips. Where did that idea come from?

  1. Its name
  2. Its place
  3. Its opposite
  4. Its output

The camera’s output (printed slips) became the page’s material.

4. A mountain hut’s site shows whether its stove is lit right now. What is that?

  1. A slop tell
  2. One live thing
  3. The signature move
  4. A Glance fact

Different on each visit, built from the hut’s real state. It’s Taste Skill’s own example of a live thing.

5. Which of these disqualifies a live thing?

  1. It uses the subject’s real content
  2. It shows something new each visit
  3. It only works with a mouse
  4. It has a plain no-script fallback

It must work on a phone with a thumb. A hover-only or pointer-only toy fails.

6. Your site’s idea: “The mountain I hike becomes the ASCII backdrop.” Another engineer who hikes could say it too. What would make it pass?

  1. Naming the actual mountain itself
  2. Making the ASCII art bigger
  3. Adding more hiking photos below
  4. Coloring the mountain bright orange

“A mountain” is a category; one named peak, from your own photo, is yours. Size, photos and color don’t change who the sentence fits.
