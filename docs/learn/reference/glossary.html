Glossary

The words this course uses, each in one or two plain sentences, with the rule that owns it. Lessons use these words and only these meanings.

Rule files live in ~/code/design-principles: T taste, P floor, C color, V views, I interaction, ST states, H usability (letters name the kind of rule; SKILL.md says which stage file holds each), B this site’s DESIGN.md. Jump to: A–C · D–F · G–L · M–P · R–S · T–Z

TermMeaningRule
AcceleratorA faster second way to do something (shortcut, deep link, batch action) that experts find and novices never trip on.H7, I6
AccentThe one loud color, kept for the action and at most one other thing. On this site, orange, under about 5% of a screen.T5, C5
Action budgetEssentials cost zero actions; everything else costs at most one. Scrolling is free; scroll-jacking isn’t.P4
Aesthetic-usability effectPeople rate attractive designs as easier to use and forgive their problems, which can hide problems in testing.H8
CardA bordered or shadowed box around one item. Right only when people pick or act on objects; otherwise a list, table or section reads better.V1, V4
clamp()A CSS size with a floor, a preferred value that grows with the screen, and a ceiling, so one headline fits a phone and a wide screen.P7, T3
Cognitive loadThe mental effort a page asks for. Every surprise, unknown word or thing to remember adds to it.H4, H6
Color vision deficiencySeeing some hues as the same. Protanopia and deuteranopia (red-green) flatten greens and oranges; lightness still shows. Simulate it in DevTools Rendering.C3, C4
Competitor testPut the nearest competitor into your idea sentence. If it still works, you’ve described the category, not the subject.T1
CompositionHow one section is laid out: statement, split, feature + list, editorial rows, index with preview, timeline, fact sheet, full-bleed picture, field chapter, table.V2
Consistency, internal and externalInternal: one meaning per word, icon and place inside a product. External: working like the rest of the web and the platform.H4
ContainerWhat holds a set of items: section, list, table, tabs, expandable, dialog, row, or cards. Picked by what the reader is doing.V1
Contrast ratioHow far apart two colors are in lightness, 1:1 to 21:1. Your floors: body 7:1, other text 4.5:1, large text and interface marks 3:1. Never rounded.C2, P2
Deliberate restThe one quiet stretch a page may have: a placed picture or a field of color. Two near-empty screens is underbuilt, not calm.T4
Editorial rowsA list aligned to shared columns, like year · title · one sentence. The usual replacement for a card grid.V2, V5
EyebrowA small uppercase, widely tracked label or pill above a heading. A slop tell: put the words in the heading or drop them.T2
FieldThe dark green ground of a whole chapter (Journey, the close). A color change instead of a box.T5, B2
Five-second testLook at the first screen for five seconds. A stranger must be able to say what it is and what to do next: both halves.P4
Glance, Scan, ExploreThe three layers of a page. Glance: the first screen, zero actions. Scan: what most people need, by scrolling. Explore: the playful part, opt-in, with a plain version.P5
GroundThe page’s main background, about 60–70% of what people see.T5, B2
HeuristicA rule of thumb for judging a design. Nielsen’s ten are the standard set; breaking one needs a reason.H1–H10
Heuristic reviewWalking a task twice and logging each place it breaks a heuristic, with a severity and a fix. Best done by 3–5 people alone, then merged.H11
Hover previewSomething shown while pointing at an item. Allowed only as a preview: focus and tap must reach the same thing.P4, V11
Idea sentence“On this page, [something that belongs to the subject] becomes [a design device].” Written before building.T1
InkThe full-strength text color, for everything people read in sentences. Gray is only for notes.T3, B2
Jakob’s LawPeople spend most of their time on other sites, so they expect yours to work like those.H4
Left edgesThe vertical lines text blocks start on. Two or three shared edges read as one page; six reads as ad hoc.T4, K10
Line spacing (leading)Space between lines, as a multiple of the font size. Running text 1.5–1.7; big headlines 0.85–1.05.T3, P7
MeasureLine length in characters. Running text 45–75, often capped with max-width: 65ch.P7
Mental modelWhat someone believes about how a thing works, built from everything they’ve used before.H2, H5
MistakeA conscious error from a wrong mental model. Fixed with clearer words and feedback, not just guard rails. Compare slip.H5
Motion layerThe quiet motion around the one wow: entrances and control feedback, all off or short under reduced motion.T8, P8
Natural mappingControls placed like their effect: increase above decrease, back on the left.H2
One live thingSomething the visitor can do, or that changes each visit, made from the idea.T1
One wowThe page’s single signature interaction. On this site, the ASCII mountain.P8
Outer marginsThe pair of gaps between content and the screen’s sides: equal, 16–32px, everywhere. Big pictures may bleed past them.T4, P6
Progressive disclosureShow the few most important things first and the rest on request. More than two levels and people get lost.P5, V7
ProximityThings near each other read as related. So a heading sits closer to its own text than to the section above.T4, H8
Pull and push helpPull help appears from the person’s context (a hint by the control). Push help interrupts regardless (launch tours) and is mostly dismissed.H10
RailIn v2, the 12rem left column holding each section’s label, beside the content. Stacks above the content on phones.B4, B5
Recognition over recallSpotting the right option is easier than remembering it. Show options, labels and recent items instead.H6
Role tokenA color named by its job (--action, --muted) instead of its value. Components use tokens; only the token block holds hex.C1
Rule strengths (MUST, SHOULD, CONSIDER, Verify)Rule strengths: required; the default unless you state a reason; an idea. Verify says how to prove it.SKILL.md
Section gapSpace between sections: clearly larger than space inside one, about 120–240px on desktop.T4
SeverityHow bad a usability problem is, 0–4: not a problem, cosmetic, minor, major, catastrophe. Judged by frequency, impact and persistence.H11
Signal and noiseSignal carries information the reader needs; noise is decoration, jargon and clutter that competes with it.H8, T2
SlipAn autopilot error, like clicking the button next to the one you meant. Experts make them too. Fixed with constraints, defaults and spacing.H5
Slop tellA habit that makes a page look generated: icon feature cards, eyebrows, gradients, glow, decorative numbering. Each has a replacement.T2
StatementOne sentence set large, about 25 words and 36px or less on desktop. Bigger reads as a headline that’s too long.T3, V2
SurfaceA small tone step from the ground that groups content without a border or shadow.T5, B2
System statusWhat the system is doing right now. People must see the result of every action, and progress for anything slow.H1, I2
TrackingLetter spacing. Big type tightens to −0.02 to −0.05em; wide tracking in caps is the eyebrow look.T3
TrailIn v2, the Peach line drawn down the Journey’s rail. Decoration only: the timeline beside it carries every fact.B4, P5
Undo and exitsBack, Close, Cancel and Undo: the clearly marked ways out of something people didn’t mean to do.H3, I5
