Frame and space

Type gave the page its voice; the frame gives it a shape. By the end you’ll set a page’s margins, left edges and gaps so it reads as one designed thing, and spot a phone layout that’s just a squeezed desktop.

One frame

Every section sits inside the same pair of outer margins: equal on both sides, 16–32px, at every width (T4). Never under 16px on a phone (P6). Big pictures may bleed past them to the screen’s edge; text never does.

Taste Skill’s inspector checks this first, and its rule is blunt: asymmetry belongs inside the grid, not in the frame (Taste Skill, “The frame”). A page shoved to one side with the other side empty reads as unfinished, not bold.

Two or three left edges

A left edge is a vertical line that text blocks start on. Every new one makes the eye search for a new start, so text blocks share two or three edges down the whole page (T4). Six is ad hoc; your own critique step asks you to count them (K10).

Your v2 rail is built for this: the section labels start on the margin, and everything else starts on the content column, 12rem in. That’s two edges below the hero (B5). Center at most one moment, like the close.

Space says what belongs together

Things near each other read as related. That’s the Gestalt principle of proximity, and NN/g finds it can beat color and shape as a grouping cue (Harley, “Proximity Principle in Visual Design”). Two consequences:

  • A heading sits closer to its own text than to the section above. Equal space above and below, and it floats between both.
  • Gaps between sections are clearly bigger than gaps inside one. On desktop that’s about 120–240px; the sites in Taste Skill’s study sat near 240px. Your draft uses clamp(96px, 14vw, 200px).

When the gaps do the grouping, you don’t need divider lines or boxes, which is why the card grid from lesson 2 was never necessary.

Full, not empty

Each desktop screen should read like a poster with one dominant thing (Taste Skill, “Fullness”). So:

  • No blank stretch taller than half a screen (T4).
  • If text fills the left half, the right half holds something: a picture, a list, a second column.
  • One deliberate rest per page, as a placed picture or a field of color. Two near-empty screens read as underbuilt, not calm.

Neighbors differ

Next-door sections use different compositions, and no layout appears three times (T4, V2). Let the content pick: a sequence wants a timeline, a standout wants a feature, a set of names wants a list. Your v2 draft already does it: Journey is a timeline on Field, Projects a big-type list with a preview, About a statement then tabs, the close one line on Field.

One frame, two or three edges, small gaps inside a section and big ones between. Every screen holds something, and no two neighbors look alike.

On a phone

Compose the phone layout for the phone; don’t squeeze the desktop one (T4, V11). Your rail stacks below 768px: each label goes above its content (B8). Watch proximity when columns stack: NN/g’s Transport for London example puts two related links side by side on desktop, then far apart on mobile, where people miss the second one.

Practice

1. A heading sits 48px below the previous section and 48px above its own text.

  1. The heading needs more weight
  2. It floats between two sections
  3. Gaps must use 8px steps
  4. Headings need a top border

Proximity: equal space ties it to both sections. Try about 160px above and 24px below.

2. On desktop the left margin is 32px and the right is 64px.

  1. Fine: asymmetry adds visual interest
  2. Widen both to sixty-four pixels
  3. Center the content column instead
  4. Make both margins the same

One equal pair everywhere, 16–32px (T4). Asymmetry goes inside the grid, never in the frame.

3. Your v2 rail gives the page two left edges. Which addition costs a new one?

  1. A pull quote indented 40px
  2. A centered closing line, once
  3. A photo bleeding past margins
  4. A list on content edge

The indent is a new start for the eye. Keep the quote on the content edge and let its size set it apart. One centered moment is allowed, pictures may bleed, and the list reuses an edge.

4. On desktop, sections sit 40px apart and paragraphs 32px apart.

  1. Shrink the paragraph gaps more
  2. Add divider lines between sections
  3. Raise section gaps toward 200px
  4. Put every section inside cards

Section gaps must be clearly bigger than inner ones: 120–240px on desktop. Lines and boxes are the slop fix for a spacing problem.

5. Your statement fills the left half of a desktop screen; the right half is empty ground.

  1. Center the statement on screen
  2. Make the font size bigger
  3. Add a gradient behind it
  4. Put a picture beside it

Each section uses the page’s width: a picture, list or second column fills the other half. A bigger statement breaks the 36px limit; a gradient is a tell.

6. Journey, Projects and About are all laid out as timelines.

  1. Neighbors need different compositions
  2. Timelines need date columns
  3. Sections each need pictures
  4. Fields must alternate colors

No layout three times, and neighbors differ (T4, V2). Projects want a big-type list; About wants a statement.

7. At 390px, the rail squeezes every section label into a 90px column.

  1. Shrink the rail label font
  2. Hide the rail labels entirely
  3. Stack labels above their content
  4. Let the page scroll sideways

Compose for the phone (T4, V11): below 768px the label sits above its content at the same size (B8).

8. From lesson 5: which body text setting passes every rule?

  1. 15px, 1.4, 80 characters
  2. 18px, 1.6, 65 characters
  3. 18px, 1.3, 65 characters
  4. 20px, 1.6, 95 characters

16–20px, line spacing 1.5–1.7 (your call), 45–75 characters a line.

9. How many quiet, near-empty stretches may one page have?

  1. None, every screen stays full
  2. Two, one at each end
  3. Just one, placed on purpose
  4. One inside every single section

One deliberate rest, as a placed picture or a field of color. A second one reads as an underbuilt page.
