Type that does the work

When the cards, eyebrows and gradients go, type has to carry the page. By the end you’ll set a body size, line spacing, line length and headline that pass every rule, and know what to cut when a headline won’t fit.

Start with the body

Matthew Butterick’s first rule: most of a page is body text, so make that look good first and worry about the rest after (“Typography in ten minutes”). Three numbers decide it:

ChoiceYour ruleWhy
Size16–20px, in rem (T3, P7)Readable without leaning in; rem lets visitors zoom. Butterick gives 15–25px for the web.
Line spacing1.5–1.7 (T3, P7)WCAG asks for at least 1.5 within paragraphs (1.4.8). Butterick prefers 1.2–1.45, but he isn’t designing for low vision.
Line length45–75 characters (P7)Long lines make the eye hunt for the next line’s start. Butterick allows up to 90.
ColorFull ink (T3)Gray running text reads unfinished; gray is for notes and captions.

Your rules used to disagree here (T3 said 1.45–1.6). On 2026-10-21 you settled it: 1.5–1.7 everywhere, so T3 now matches P7 and nothing drops below WCAG’s floor.

Then make the headline truly large

Taste Skill’s study measured the gap: the median headline was 4.3× the body size, and the largest type on top sites was much bigger than on weak ones (8.4vw against 5.0vw) (Taste Skill, “Laws of the craft”). Middle sizes blur the hierarchy; two or three sizes do the visible work, and five is the ceiling.

  • Ratio: headline at least 3× body; about 4× is strong. Your hero runs 144px over 18px: 8×.
  • Tracking and leading: big type looks loose at default spacing. Tighten letter spacing to −0.02 to −0.05em and line spacing to 0.85–1.05.
  • Two lines, at every width. When it won’t fit, cut words first, then widen the column, and shrink the size last.
  • Scale with the screen. A 144px name can’t fit a 320px phone, so big sizes use clamp(): your hero is clamp(3.5rem, 11vw, 9rem), 56px on a small phone up to 144px on a wide screen, and always in rem so zoom still works (P7).
  • Statements are paragraphs. A sentence set large stays about 25 words and, per Taste Skill, 36px or less on desktop. Above that, readers take a four-line sentence as a four-line headline.

Make the body comfortable, make the headline huge, and skip everything in between. If a headline breaks to three lines, cut words before you touch the size.

Try it

Start from Broken start and make all five checks pass. Then try Your v2 statement: at 64px, how many lines does a 24-word sentence take? What size keeps it at three? (A statement is allowed three lines, so ignore the two-line check for that one.)

Best on a laptop: a phone screen is too narrow for 45 characters a line at 18px, which is exactly why phones get their own sizes.

Practice

1. Your headline breaks to three lines on a phone. What do you try first?

  1. Cut words from the headline
  2. Shrink the headline font size
  3. Tighten the headline line height
  4. Hyphenate the longest headline word

Words first, then a wider column, size last. A shorter headline is usually a better headline anyway.

2. Body at 18px, headline at 36px.

  1. Fine: the ratio matters little
  2. Body too small: under 16px
  3. Headline too small: under 3×
  4. Headline too big: over 3×

36 ÷ 18 = 2×. The headline should be at least 54px, and around 72px puts it where strong sites are.

3. Paragraphs run edge to edge at 110 characters a line.

  1. Raise the body font size
  2. Add space between the paragraphs
  3. Switch to a monospace face
  4. Cap the column near 65ch

Long lines are a column-width problem: max-width: 65ch on the text block. Raising the size would help a little, by accident.

4. Butterick prefers line spacing of 1.2–1.45. Why does your rule ask for 1.5 or more?

  1. Serif fonts always need more space
  2. WCAG asks for 1.5 in paragraphs
  3. Screens render lines closer than paper
  4. Butterick only writes about print documents

WCAG 1.4.8 asks for space-and-a-half within paragraphs, for readers with low vision or dyslexia. Your floor is set by access, not taste.

5. Your body text is set in a soft gray for a lighter look.

  1. Gray text loads slower on phones
  2. Gray breaks the five-size limit rule
  3. Gray is reserved for link colors
  4. Running text reads unfinished in gray

Full ink for everything you read in sentences; gray only for notes and captions (T3).

6. At most, how many type sizes on one page?

  1. Three sizes
  2. Seven sizes
  3. Five sizes
  4. Nine sizes

Five is the ceiling; usually one very large and one small do most of the work.

7. Your 144px name uses the font’s default letter spacing.

  1. Tighten it to about −0.03em
  2. Loosen it to about 0.1em
  3. Uppercase it and track wide
  4. Leave it at zero spacing

Big type looks gappy at default spacing; −0.02 to −0.05em pulls it together. Wide tracking in caps is the eyebrow look you already ban.

8. The v2 draft sets the About me statement at up to 64px. What does Taste Skill’s finding say?

  1. Four lines is ideal for statements
  2. Over 36px it reads as headline
  3. Statements must always be set italic
  4. Statements need a gold highlight color

Jurors read a large four-line sentence as a four-line headline, which breaks the two-line rule. The draft should cap statements near 36px.
